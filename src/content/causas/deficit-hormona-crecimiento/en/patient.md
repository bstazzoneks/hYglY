<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: fasting.</strong> Risk is higher in children and hypopituitarism.</aside>
## What it is
The pituitary makes too little growth hormone.
## Quick summary
- It may occur during fasting.
- Pediatric risk requires specialist review.
- ACTH deficit may coexist.
- Stimulated testing is needed.
- Replacement requires supervision.
- No doses are listed here.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, confusion and fatigue.
## Why it happens
Low growth hormone reduces metabolic responses and reserves.
## Who may have it
Children or adults with hypopituitarism, tumors or surgery.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose and review pituitary hormones with specialist testing.
## Treatment
Replacement only under endocrine supervision.
## Food and eating
Regular meals without prolonged fasting.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose if able to swallow and recheck after 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Are there other pituitary deficits?
- Do I need stimulated testing?
- Which plan applies to children?
## More information
Pediatric or adult endocrine teams.
