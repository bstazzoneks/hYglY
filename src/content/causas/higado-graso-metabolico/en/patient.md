<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> Frank hypoglycemia may occur with advanced liver disease, not mild fat accumulation.</aside>
## What it is
It is fat accumulation in the liver associated with metabolic dysfunction.
## Quick summary
- It often relates to insulin resistance.
- Advanced damage may alter glucose.
- Symptoms require measurement.
- Alcohol and medicines require review.
- Hypoglycemia may occur with cirrhosis.
- Management depends on disease stage.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger or confusion.
## Why it happens
Resistance may increase insulin. Advanced disease may reduce liver glucose reserves.
## Who may have it
People with metabolic syndrome, diabetes or obesity.
## How it is diagnosed
Confirm low glucose and assess liver function. Exclude alcohol, medicines and kidney failure.
## Treatment
Metabolic control and hepatology review according to damage.
## Food and eating
Regular meals and attention to liver nutrition. Avoid alcohol if damaged or as advised.
## What to do during an episode
If conscious and glucose is low, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, seizures or persistent confusion.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms. Plan exercise with the team.
## Questions for your doctor
- Is there cirrhosis or advanced damage?
- Do symptoms match low glucose?
- Which medicines affect glucose?
## More information
Hepatology and endocrine care.
