<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> Obesity alone does not cause hypoglycemia.</aside>
## What it is
Excess adiposity may alter insulin and postprandial hormone responses.
## Quick summary
- Resistance and hyperinsulinism may occur.
- Symptoms need glucose measurement.
- Surgery may change the mechanism.
- Medicines require review.
- Activity and food help metabolic control.
- Avoid extreme restrictive diets.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, fatigue or confusion.
## Why it happens
Tissue responds less to insulin. The pancreas may produce more and a later fall may occur.
## Who may have it
People with central obesity, diabetes or metabolic syndrome.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose and review surgery or treatment history. Apply Whipple's triad when episodes are documented.
## Treatment
Individual metabolic goals, activity and food. Bariatric surgery requires specialized follow-up.
## Food and eating
Regular meals, reasonable portions and carbohydrate with protein and fiber.
## What to do during an episode
If conscious and glucose is low, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, seizures or persistent confusion.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms. Plan gradual exercise.
## Questions for your doctor
- Do I have insulin resistance?
- Do symptoms match low glucose?
- Could surgery or medicine affect episodes?
## More information
Primary care and metabolic specialists.
