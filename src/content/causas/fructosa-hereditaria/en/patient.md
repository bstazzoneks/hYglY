<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: postprandial.</strong> It may follow fructose, sucrose or sorbitol exposure.</aside>
## What it is
A liver enzyme does not process fructose. Metabolites build up and block glucose production.
## Quick summary
- Food exposure triggers symptoms.
- Vomiting, hypoglycemia or liver injury may occur.
- Fructose, sucrose and sorbitol must be avoided.
- Metabolic evaluation confirms it.
- Never perform a challenge outside supervision.
- Refer to pediatric or metabolic care.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, confusion, vomiting or sleepiness after eating.
## Why it happens
The liver accumulates metabolites and cannot release glucose.
## Who may have it
Children, although adults with late diagnosis may present.
## How it is diagnosed
History, metabolic tests and specialized genetic testing. Deliberate exposure is not recommended outside supervision.
## Treatment
Avoid fructose, sucrose and sorbitol. Treat hypoglycemia urgently.
## Food and eating
Use food plans reviewed by a metabolic team. Medicines or syrups may contain related sugar.
## What to do during an episode
If able to swallow, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for persistent vomiting, dehydration, seizures or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms and carry a safe food and medicine list.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which substances must I avoid?
- Do I need genetic testing?
- Which emergency plan applies?
## More information
Specialized inherited metabolic disorders teams.
