<aside class="medical-type">
<strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong>
A rapid postprandial decline is not necessarily hypoglycemia. Evidence for a primary idiopathic entity is limited.
</aside>

## Clinical summary

This category includes postprandial symptoms without a demonstrated organic cause.

It should not replace systematic evaluation. Whipple's triad should be fulfilled.

The main priority is to exclude true hypoglycemia and conditions mimicking its symptoms.

## Definition

True hypoglycemia requires compatible symptoms and low glucose during the episode. Symptoms should improve after glucose rises.

Without simultaneous low glucose, the appropriate diagnosis is unexplained or functional symptoms. This avoids overdiagnosis.

## Epidemiology

No reliable prevalence exists for a primary idiopathic entity. Historical estimates included symptomatic people without biochemical measurement.

The category remains controversial in adults and should rely on contemporary data.

## Pathophysiology

Absorption, insulin response and incretin response may vary. No single mechanism is demonstrated.

A rapid decline from a postprandial peak can produce symptoms. This differs from confirmed hypoglycemia and requires measurement.

## Clinical presentation

| Pattern | Considerations |
|---|---|
| 2 to 4 hours after meals | May reflect true hypoglycemia or physiologic variation |
| Early postprandial | Suggests rapid absorption, anxiety or dumping |
| Symptoms without low glucose | Raises priority for functional diagnosis |
| Night episodes | Prompt review of endogenous or exposure-related hypoglycemia |

## Diagnostic criteria

Apply Whipple's triad.

Record symptoms and low glucose simultaneously with a valid laboratory method. Confirm improvement after glucose rises.

Do not interpret one isolated casual reading as diagnostic. Review drug exposure and measurement errors.

## Tests and cutoff values

No dedicated cutoff values are listed because no universally validated category-specific threshold is cited here.

| Test | Clinical use | Cautious interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Point-of-care glucose | Detects potential episodes | May differ from laboratory glucose |
| Serum glucose | Confirms the critical measurement | Must coincide with symptoms |
| Insulin and C-peptide | Exclude endogenous hypersecretion | Do not rely on random measurements |
| Sulfonylurea screening | Excludes inadvertent exposure | Availability varies |
| Mixed meal test | Studies postprandial response | Requires specialist protocol |
| Supervised fast | Excludes inter-meal hypoglycemia | Protocols vary by guideline |

## Differential diagnosis

Post-surgical reactive hypoglycemia, insulinoma, insulin or secretagogue exposure, alcohol and IGF tumors.

Anxiety, arrhythmias, postural tachycardia, pheochromocytoma, hyperventilation and anemia. Also physiologic postprandial responses and autonomic dysfunction.

## Management

Prioritize confirmation before metabolic treatment. Avoid restrictive diets based only on symptoms.

If hypoglycemia is not confirmed, manage coexisting factors and refer for mental health support where appropriate. Confirmed hypoglycemia should follow its cause.

Do not recommend specific pharmacotherapy without etiologic diagnosis. Review diet, activity and occupational safety.

## Complications and prognosis

The major risk is misattribution or delayed diagnosis of an alternative condition. Unnecessary food restriction may also occur.

If true hypoglycemia is confirmed, prognosis depends on the cause. Without confirmation, prognosis relates to the alternative explanation.

## Follow-up

Compare symptoms with simultaneous measurements. Stop interpreting home monitors if analytical validity is inadequate.

Refer to endocrinology for reproducible low glucose or an incomplete triad with meaningful risk. Reassess drugs and prior surgery.

## Special populations

In pregnancy or breastfeeding, refer before prolonged fasting. In children, prioritize inherited metabolic causes.

In older adults, consider medicines, renal impairment and frailty. Adjust driving and fall precautions.
