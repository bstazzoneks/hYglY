<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: fasting.</strong> Postprandial presentation is uncommon.</aside>
## What it is
Large extra-pancreatic tumors may produce incomplete IGF-2.
## Quick summary
- Episodes usually occur during fasting.
- Abdominal or retroperitoneal masses may exist.
- Insulin is usually low or suppressed.
- Insulinoma must be excluded.
- Tumor surgery may control it.
- Hypoglycemia may persist until tumor control.
## Symptoms
Weakness, confusion, sweating and loss of consciousness.
## Why it happens
IGF-2 activates insulin-like signals and increases glucose use.
## Who may have it
People with large mesenchymal, hepatic or adrenal tumors.
## How it is diagnosed
Critical glucose, low insulin, C-peptide, IGF-2 testing and imaging.
## Treatment
Remove the tumor when possible. Medical glucose support may be needed.
## Food and eating
Frequent meals and rapid glucose while tumor control is pursued.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose if able and recheck.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness or seizures.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms and coordinate oncology and endocrine care.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which tumor produces IGF-2?
- Is insulin suppressed?
- Can the tumor be removed?
## More information
Oncology and endocrine specialty teams.
