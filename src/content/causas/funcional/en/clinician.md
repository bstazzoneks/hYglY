<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> Many cases do not meet biochemical criteria.</aside>

## Clinical summary
Do not attribute postprandial symptoms to hypoglycemia without simultaneous low glucose.

## Definition
A normal reading or rapid postprandial decline is insufficient. Document Whipple's triad.

## Epidemiology
Prevalence is undefined. The category may include heterogeneous functional symptoms.

## Pathophysiology
Autonomic and insulin responses vary after meals. The exact mechanism is not demonstrated.

## Clinical presentation
Symptoms after eating without low glucose. It overlaps anxiety, arrhythmia and dumping.

## Diagnostic criteria
Apply Whipple's triad with a valid simultaneous sample. Do not use isolated home readings.

## Tests and cutoff values
No dedicated cutoff is listed.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Critical serum glucose | Confirms simultaneous low value |
| Insulin and C-peptide | Excludes hyperinsulinism |
| Supervised test | Only if it changes clinical decisions |

## Differential diagnosis
Anxiety, physiologic decline, dumping, insulinoma, medicines and anemia.

## Management
Confirm etiology. Treat coexisting factors and avoid unjustified food restriction.

## Complications and prognosis
Food restriction, fear and delayed alternative diagnoses.

## Follow-up
Record timing and valid measurements. Refer for reproducible low glucose.

## Special populations
Adapt for pregnancy, pediatric patients and older adults.
