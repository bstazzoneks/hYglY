<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: both.</strong> It may follow sweet drinks or prolonged fasting.</aside>
## What it is
Alcohol blocks liver glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis.
## Quick summary
- Fasting increases risk.
- Sweet mixers may create variable postprandial patterns.
- It may resemble intoxication.
- Glucose measurement is essential.
- Do not drive after drinking or with symptoms.
## Symptoms
Sleepiness, confusion, trembling and loss of consciousness.
## Why it happens
Alcohol metabolism lowers liver glucose production.
## Who may have it
People who fast, have malnutrition, diabetes or frequent alcohol exposure.
## How it is diagnosed
Critical glucose and review of exposure, insulin and medicines.
## Treatment
Stop exposure and restore glucose under supervision. Prevent it with food.
## Food and eating
Avoid alcohol without food if advised by the team. Avoid fasting.
## What to do during an episode
Measure glucose and take rapid glucose if conscious.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, seizures or persistent confusion.
## Daily life
Do not drive or swim after drinking. Discuss alcohol with your clinician.
## Questions for your doctor
- Could my symptoms be hypoglycemia?
- What risk do alcohol and fasting create?
- Which plan applies with diabetes?
