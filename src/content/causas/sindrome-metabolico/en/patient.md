<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> Symptoms do not prove hypoglycemia.</aside>

## What it is
It includes abdominal obesity, high blood pressure, high triglycerides and abnormal glucose. Insulin resistance may precede diabetes.

## Quick summary
- Hyperinsulinism may occur.
- Symptoms require measurement.
- Food and activity influence glucose.
- Fatigue alone does not confirm hypoglycemia.
- Treatment improves metabolic factors.
- Alternative causes must be excluded.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger or confusion. Fatigue, thirst or dizziness may have other explanations.

## Why it happens
Muscle responds less to insulin. The pancreas makes more insulin and a later fall may occur.

## Who may have it
People with central obesity, high blood pressure or family history have higher risk.

## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose during symptoms and complete metabolic tests. Apply Whipple's triad when suspicion remains.

## Treatment
Activity, food and blood pressure and lipid control. Medicines require assessment.

## Food and eating
Regular meals with measured carbohydrate. Avoid excess sugar and calorie-rich drinks.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious and glucose is low, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek care for loss of consciousness, seizures or persistent confusion.

## Daily life
Plan gradual activity and safe driving. Avoid alcohol on an empty stomach.

## Questions for your doctor
- Do I have insulin resistance?
- Did low glucose coincide with symptoms?
- Which metabolic test do I need?

## More information
Primary care and cardiovascular prevention.
