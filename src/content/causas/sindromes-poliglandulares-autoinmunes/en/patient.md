<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type produced most often: fasting.</strong> Postprandial hypoglycemia may occur with medicines or adrenal failure.</aside>
## What it is
Combinations of autoimmune diseases affecting several glands.
## Quick summary
- Addison disease, hypoparathyroidism, thyroiditis or diabetes may coexist.
- Low glucose and dehydration may occur.
- Endocrine evaluation should be complete.
- Do not stop corticosteroids.
- Symptoms require measurement.
- Treatment replaces missing hormones.
## Symptoms
Weakness, nausea, darkened skin, weight loss and confusion.
## Why it happens
Cortisol, glucagon or other hormone loss reduces counter-regulation.
## Who may have it
People with family history or several autoimmune diseases.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose and review cortisol, ACTH, thyroid, calcium and antibodies.
## Treatment
Supervised hormone replacement and stress or infection management.
## Food and eating
Regular meals without fasting. Follow specific cortisol and diabetes plans.
## What to do during an episode
If conscious and glucose is low, take rapid glucose. Do not omit prescribed hormones.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, abdominal pain, dehydration or confusion.
## Daily life
Carry a medical card and sick-day plan. Avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which glands are affected?
- Do I need emergency cortisol?
- Is there hypoparathyroidism or diabetes?
## More information
Specialized endocrine teams.
