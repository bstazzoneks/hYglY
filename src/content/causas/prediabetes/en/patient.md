<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> An exaggerated insulin response may occur before diabetes develops.</aside>

## What it is
Prediabetes has glucose above normal but not enough for a diabetes diagnosis. Some people have symptoms during a postprandial decline.

## Quick summary
- Insulin resistance may be present.
- Measurements must match symptoms.
- Not every symptom means hypoglycemia.
- Activity and food influence glucose.
- Tumors and medicines should be excluded.
- Primary care should review progression.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, fatigue or trouble concentrating.

## Why it happens
Insulin may lag or anticipate glucose entry. This may cause a rapid fall after eating.

## Who may have it
People with obesity, family history or metabolic syndrome may have higher risk.

## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose during symptoms and apply Whipple's triad. Confirm or exclude diabetes with standard tests.

## Treatment
Management usually focuses on regular food, activity and metabolic control. Medicines are used only when indicated.

## Food and eating
Prefer reasonable portions and regular timing. Combine carbohydrate with fiber and protein.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes. Recheck and repeat once if still low.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek care for loss of consciousness, seizures or persistent confusion.

## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms. Plan exercise and alcohol with the care team.

## Questions for your doctor
- Do symptoms match low glucose?
- Do I have prediabetes or diabetes?
- Which test do I need?
- Which food changes are safe?

## More information
Primary care, endocrinology and diabetes society guidance.
