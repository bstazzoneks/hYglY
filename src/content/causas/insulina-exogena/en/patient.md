<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: both.</strong> It may occur after meals, fasting or exercise.</aside>
## What it is
Insulin given externally lowers glucose beyond need.
## Quick summary
- Food, exercise or fasting affect timing.
- Glucose should be measured during symptoms.
- Adjustment requires a care team.
- C-peptide may distinguish origin.
- Injection and storage should be reviewed.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, confusion and loss of consciousness.
## Why it happens
Insulin increases glucose uptake and suppresses liver production.
## Who may have it
People using insulin for diabetes or another indication.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose, insulin and C-peptide. Review injection and medicines.
## Treatment
Adjust type, timing and food with the team. Never change dose alone.
## Food and eating
Keep planned meal timing and rapid carbohydrate available.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes. Use glucagon if directed.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, seizures or inability to swallow.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms and keep a written plan.
## Questions for your doctor
- Does insulin match meals and exercise?
- Could C-peptide confirm origin?
- Do I need preparation or timing adjustment?
## More information
Diabetes team and therapeutic education.
