<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type produced most often: fasting.</strong> Postprandial episodes may occur but are not the dominant pattern.</aside>
## What it is
A rare tumor of pancreatic beta cells. It produces insulin and may lower glucose.
## Quick summary
- Episodes often occur while fasting or between meals.
- Trembling, sweating, confusion or seizures may occur.
- Critical sampling and special tests confirm it.
- Medicines and factitious hypoglycemia must be excluded.
- Surgery may be curative in some cases.
- Specialist study is required.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, palpitations and confusion.
## Why it happens
The tumor releases insulin when glucose should not fall.
## Who may have it
People with recurrent fasting episodes or loss of consciousness.
## How it is diagnosed
Supervised fasting with glucose, insulin, C-peptide and drug screening.
## Treatment
Surgery may remove the tumor. Medicines may be used while preparing surgery.
## Food and eating
Frequent meals may reduce episodes during evaluation. Do not delay specialist assessment.
## What to do during an episode
If able to swallow, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures, loss of consciousness or persistent confusion.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms and carry rapid glucose.
## Questions for your doctor
- Did episodes occur during fasting?
- Did critical testing confirm endogenous insulin?
- Which imaging is needed?
- Am I a surgical candidate?
## More information
Specialized endocrine and surgical teams.
