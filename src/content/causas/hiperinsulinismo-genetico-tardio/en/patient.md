<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> Timing depends on protein, fasting or exercise for each variant.</aside>

## What it is
Some variants affect beta-cell channels or enzymes. They may cause inappropriate insulin release.

ABCC8, KCNJ11, GCK, GLUD1, HNF1A, HNF4A, UCP2 and SLC16A1 are related genes. Not every variant causes postprandial hypoglycemia.

## Quick summary
- Some variants remain unrecognized until adulthood.
- Patterns vary by gene and trigger.
- Exercise may trigger SLC16A1 disease.
- Protein may trigger GLUD1 disease.
- Glucose and genetic studies confirm it.
- Genetic counseling may help.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger and confusion. Seizures or loss of consciousness may occur.

Symptoms should coincide with low glucose.

## Why it happens
Beta cells release insulin inappropriately. Food, fasting or exercise may act as triggers.

The relationship differs across variants.

## Who may have it
Family history or previous childhood diagnosis may exist. Some adults receive the diagnosis after recurrent episodes.

Genetic testing requires specialist evaluation.

## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose, insulin, C-peptide and beta-hydroxybutyrate during hypoglycemia. Exclude medicines and tumors.

Genetic counseling helps interpret family studies. Some results remain uncertain.

## Treatment
Treatment depends on gene and pattern. It may include food adjustment, planned exercise and supervised medicines.

Do not change medicines without specialist guidance.

## Food and eating
Avoid prolonged fasting if episodes relate to fasting. Some variants need specific plans.

Do not start restrictive diets alone. A dietitian can adapt protein and carbohydrate intake.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes. Repeat once if glucose remains low.

Call emergency services for unconsciousness or seizure.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures, loss of consciousness or persistent confusion.

## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms or low glucose. Plan exercise and work with the care team.

Pregnancy requires specialized referral.

## Questions for your doctor
- Which trigger matches my episodes?
- Do I need genetic testing?
- Could a result be uncertain?
- What plan applies to exercise or fasting?
- Should family members be evaluated?

## More information
- Metabolic endocrine specialists.
- Genetic counseling services.
- Pediatric or family services when appropriate.
