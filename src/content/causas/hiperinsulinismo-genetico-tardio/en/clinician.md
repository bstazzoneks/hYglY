<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> Exercise, protein or fasting may trigger episodes depending on variant.</aside>

## Clinical summary
Monogenic disorders alter pancreatic insulin secretion.

They require critical sampling and specialist genetic evaluation.

## Definition
ABCC8 and KCNJ11 alter the K_ATP channel. GCK changes glucose sensing. GLUD1 may relate to hyperammonemia.

SLC16A1 may relate to exercise-induced hypoglycemia.

## Epidemiology
These disorders are rare and adult phenotypes may remain unrecognized.

## Pathophysiology
Beta cells release insulin despite inadequate glucose. The stimulus depends on the gene.

## Clinical presentation
| Variant | Potential trigger |
|---|---|
| ABCC8 or KCNJ11 | Fasting or postprandial |
| GCK | Persistent or variable |
| GLUD1 | Protein |
| SLC16A1 | Exercise |

## Diagnostic criteria
Document endogenous hypoglycemia with insulin and C-peptide elevation. Measure ketones and glucagon response per protocol.

## Tests and cutoff values
No proprietary genetic cutoff values are listed. Specialist protocols are required.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Critical sample | Confirms endogenous hyperinsulinism |
| Ammonia | Supports GLUD1 when relevant |
| Genetic testing | Confirms causal variant |
| Supervised fast | Characterizes phenotype |

## Differential diagnosis
Insulinoma, medicines, factitious hypoglycemia, NIPHS and IGF tumors.

## Management
Individualize by phenotype and genetic pathway. Diet, controlled exercise and specialist medicines may be needed.

## Complications and prognosis
Seizures, pediatric neurodevelopmental injury and hypoglycemia-related harm.

## Follow-up
Monitor glucose and pediatric growth. Review families through genetic counseling.

## Special populations
Adapt for pregnancy and adulthood; refer pediatric cases.
