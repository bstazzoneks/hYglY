<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> A late insulin peak may follow eating.</aside>

## What it is
Early type 2 diabetes may alternate insulin resistance and hyperinsulinism. This may cause a postprandial fall without medicine exposure.

## Quick summary
- Episodes may occur after meals.
- Medicines may be the main factor.
- Measurements must match symptoms.
- Insulin and C-peptide may be reviewed.
- Do not stop treatment yourself.
- Management requires individual review.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, confusion or weakness.

## Why it happens
Insulin response may occur early or remain prolonged. Carbohydrate absorption may also vary.

## Who may have it
People with recent diabetes, rapid weight change or secretagogue use may be more exposed.

## How it is diagnosed
Document low glucose and symptoms. Review food, insulin and sulfonylurea exposure.

## Treatment
Adjust the confirmed cause with the care team. Medicine changes require supervision.

## Food and eating
Keep regular timing. Combine food and avoid large sugar loads.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness, seizures or inability to eat.

## Daily life
Plan driving, work and sport with the team. Do not change insulin alone.

## Questions for your doctor
- Is medicine or metabolism responsible?
- Which glucose value defines the episode?
- Do I need treatment adjustment?
