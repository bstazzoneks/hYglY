<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: fasting.</strong> It is rare and requires exclusion of common causes.</aside>
## What it is
Counter-regulatory response may be limited.
## Quick summary
- Glucagon and adrenaline raise glucose.
- Specific evidence is limited.
- Real episodes must be measured.
- Insulin and medicines must be excluded.
- Treatment is individual.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, confusion and weakness.
## Why it happens
The liver receives insufficient signals to release glucose.
## Who may have it
People with pancreatic damage, hypopituitarism or autonomic neuropathy.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure critical glucose and review counter-regulatory hormones.
## Treatment
Treat the primary cause and plan episodes.
## Food and eating
Regular meals and avoid fasting.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose if conscious and recheck.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness or seizures.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- What limits my response?
- Do I have glucagon available?
- Which tests confirm deficit?
## More information
Specialized endocrine care.
