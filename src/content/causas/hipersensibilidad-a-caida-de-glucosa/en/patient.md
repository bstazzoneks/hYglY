<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> Symptoms can occur without true hypoglycemia.</aside>

## What it is
It is sensitivity to a rapid glucose decline. It does not confirm hypoglycemia.

## Quick summary
- Symptoms may follow a meal.
- Glucose may be normal or declining.
- Whipple's triad is incomplete without low glucose.
- Anxiety, arrhythmia and dumping require review.
- A symptom record helps clinicians.
- Do not start restrictive diets.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger or dizziness. Fatigue and trouble concentrating may occur.

## Why it happens
Hormonal or autonomic responses may detect a rapid change. The exact direction is not always known.

## Who may have it
It may occur without diabetes or after gastric surgery. Frequent episodes require evaluation.

## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose during symptoms. Record improvement and exclude dumping, anxiety and endocrine disease.

## Treatment
Management depends on measurements and alternative causes. Regular meals or psychological support may be considered.

## Food and eating
Avoid very large or very sweet meals if they coincide with symptoms. Keep reasonable portions and timing.

## What to do during an episode
Measure glucose. If low, use the adapted 15-15 rule.

Call emergency services for unconsciousness or seizures.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for persistent confusion, loss of consciousness or injury.

## Daily life
Do not drive during symptoms. Tell your clinician about exercise and alcohol.

## Questions for your doctor
- Was glucose low or only declining?
- Could this be dumping?
- Are palpitations or anxiety present?
- Which measurements are valid?
- Which treatment is appropriate?

## More information
Primary care or endocrine assessment.
