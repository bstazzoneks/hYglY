<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> It is not hypoglycemia without simultaneous low glucose.</aside>

## Clinical summary
Characterize the decline and exclude true hypoglycemia.

## Definition
Symptoms timed to a rapid decline without confirmed low threshold.

## Epidemiology
Prevalence is undefined.

## Pathophysiology
Possible autonomic or catecholamine response to postprandial variation. Evidence is limited.

## Clinical presentation
Postprandial symptoms with glucose declining from a peak or remaining normal.

## Diagnostic criteria
Whipple's triad is incomplete if low glucose is absent. Document simultaneous measurement and improvement.

## Tests and cutoff values
No dedicated cutoff is listed.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Serial glucose | Characterizes direction and magnitude |
| ECG or Holter | Assesses arrhythmia when relevant |
| Supervised test | Only with clear suspicion |

## Differential diagnosis
Anxiety, arrhythmia, syncope, dumping, true hypoglycemia and pheochromocytoma.

## Management
Treat alternative causes. Consider small meals without extreme restriction.

## Complications and prognosis
Food restriction and unsafe driving if symptoms persist.

## Follow-up
Record symptoms and measurements. Refer for reproducible hypoglycemia.

## Special populations
Adapt for pregnancy, pediatric patients and older adults.
