<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia it may produce: both.</strong> It may alternate insulin-resistant hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia.</aside>
## What it is
The immune system makes antibodies against the insulin receptor.
## Quick summary
- It is rare.
- Glucose may alternate high and low.
- It associates with lupus, Sjogren disease, scleroderma and other autoimmune disease.
- Symptoms alone are insufficient.
- Receptor antibodies should be measured.
- Treatment is specialized.
## Symptoms
Thirst and high glucose, or trembling, sweating and confusion during low glucose.
## Why it happens
Antibodies may block or stimulate the insulin receptor.
## Who may have it
People with lupus, Sjogren disease, scleroderma or another autoimmune disease. Gammopathies may occur.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure both glucose phases and seek insulin receptor antibodies. Exclude exogenous insulin and tumors.
## Treatment
Specialist immune control and endocrine glucose management.
## Food and eating
Follow plans for hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia. Do not start diets without review.
## What to do during an episode
If conscious and glucose is low, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for severe hyperglycemia, loss of consciousness or seizures.
## Daily life
Record both phases and avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Do antibodies affect my receptor?
- Am I alternating phases?
- Which autoimmune disease is present?
## More information
Endocrine and rheumatology teams.
