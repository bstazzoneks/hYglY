## What it is
Several conditions produce trembling, palpitations, anxiety or dizziness.
## Quick summary
- Symptoms do not confirm hypoglycemia.
- Glucose should be measured during episodes.
- Heart rhythm and posture require review.
- Pheochromocytoma may cause severe episodes.
- Anxiety may coexist with true hypoglycemia.
## Symptoms
Trembling, palpitations, sweating, dizziness or headache.
## Why it happens
Autonomic response, arrhythmia or blood pressure may alter perception.
## Who may have it
People with anxiety, POTS, arrhythmia or rare adrenal disease.
## How it is diagnosed
Simultaneous measurement, ECG, orthostatic pressure and hormone testing when relevant.
## Treatment
Depends on confirmed alternative.
## Food and eating
Regular meals without unnecessary restriction.
## What to do during an episode
Measure glucose. If low, use the adapted 15-15 rule.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for chest pain, fainting, persistent rapid rhythm or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with dizziness or palpitations.
## Questions for your doctor
- Was glucose low?
- Do I have arrhythmia or POTS?
- Which test excludes pheochromocytoma?
## More information
Primary care, cardiology and mental health services.
