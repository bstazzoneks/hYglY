<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> Early dumping is mainly vascular and intestinal. Late dumping can cause true hypoglycemia.</aside>

## What it is
Dumping follows surgery that changes stomach emptying. It may follow gastrectomy, bypass, vagotomy, pyloroplasty or fundoplication.

Early dumping occurs soon after eating. Late dumping may cause hypoglycemia one to three hours later.

## Quick summary
- It may follow several gastric operations.
- Early dumping does not always mean low glucose.
- Late dumping can cause true hypoglycemia.
- Sugary drinks may worsen symptoms.
- Testing seeks the timing pattern.
- Treatment starts with food changes.

## Symptoms
Early symptoms include nausea, bloating, sweating, dizziness and diarrhea. Late symptoms include trembling, hunger and confusion.

Measure glucose if late symptoms coincide with a valid test.

## Why it happens
Food reaches the intestine quickly and changes fluids and hormones. This can cause symptoms without true hypoglycemia.

A late hormone response may release too much insulin. The combination may lower glucose.

## Who may have it
It may affect people after bariatric surgery or gastrectomy. It may also follow vagotomy, pyloroplasty or fundoplication.

No operation carries exactly the same risk.

## How it is diagnosed
A clinician reviews timing, symptoms and glucose readings. A mixed-meal test may confirm the pattern.

Whipple's triad distinguishes true hypoglycemia. Medicines and pancreatic tumors are excluded when relevant.

## Treatment
Management often starts with small, frequent meals. Liquids and carbohydrate concentration may need adjustment.

Medicines or procedures require specialist evaluation. Do not take medicines without direction.

## Food and eating
Avoid sugary liquids during meals. Separate drinks from food if advised.

Prefer protein and fiber. Eat small portions and increase intake slowly.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious, measure glucose. If low, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes.

Repeat once if still low according to the plan. Call emergency services for unconsciousness or seizures.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for persistent confusion, loss of consciousness or inability to eat.

## Daily life
Do not drive during symptoms. Carry a rapid glucose source.

Discuss alcohol, exercise and work with the care team. Pregnancy requires specialized follow-up.

## Questions for your doctor
- Is this early or late dumping?
- Do measurements confirm hypoglycemia?
- Which operation changed digestion?
- Which foods should I avoid?
- Do I need surgical or endocrine referral?

## More information
- The surgical team that performed the operation.
- An endocrine specialist.
