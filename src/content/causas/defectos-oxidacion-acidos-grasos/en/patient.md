<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: fasting.</strong> This is a metabolic differential diagnosis, not routine postprandial disease.</aside>
## What it is
The body cannot use fat for energy during fasting.
## Quick summary
- Risk rises with fasting or infection.
- Muscle pain or weakness may occur.
- Hypoglycemia may have low ketones.
- Neonatal detection may help.
- Avoid prolonged fasting.
- Pediatric or adult specialist plans are needed.
## Symptoms
Fatigue, vomiting, hypoglycemia, muscle pain or confusion.
## Why it happens
Fat oxidation failure reduces energy and ketone production.
## Who may have it
Children or adults with metabolic diagnosis or severe illness.
## How it is diagnosed
Critical laboratory testing, acylcarnitine profile and genetics.
## Treatment
Avoid fasting and treat illness. Use rapid glucose if directed.
## Food and eating
Regular meals and specific metabolic plan.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose if conscious and recheck.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for confusion, seizures, rapid breathing or severe weakness.
## Daily life
Prepare an illness plan and avoid fasting.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which defect do I have?
- What fasting interval is safe?
- Which plan applies during infection?
