<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: both.</strong> It may follow milk exposure or occur with illness and fasting.</aside>
## What it is
An enzyme is missing for galactose processing. Exposure may injure liver and other organs.
## Quick summary
- Milk or dairy exposure may trigger it.
- Illness or fasting may worsen glucose.
- Neonatal diagnosis should be confirmed.
- Diet needs specialist follow-up.
- Do not perform loading tests.
- Refer to metabolic and nutrition care.
## Symptoms
Vomiting, fatigue, confusion or hypoglycemia.
## Why it happens
Accumulation alters liver metabolism and may reduce glucose output.
## Who may have it
People with neonatal or late diagnosis and specific variants.
## How it is diagnosed
Neonatal testing, genetics and specialized metabolic studies.
## Treatment
Restriction according to type and metabolic team. Treat hypoglycemia and infections.
## Food and eating
Avoid milk as directed. Needs vary and require specialist review.
## What to do during an episode
If able to swallow, take rapid glucose and recheck after 15 minutes.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures, persistent vomiting or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Carry a food plan and avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which variant do I have?
- Which foods must I avoid?
- Which plan applies during illness?
