## What it is
Hypoglycemia caused by known or hidden insulin or secretagogue exposure.
## Quick summary
- Exposure may be accidental or deliberate.
- Insulin and C-peptide patterns differ.
- Sulfonylurea screening matters.
- The team should preserve safety.
- Do not judge before confirmation.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, confusion and loss of consciousness.
## Why it happens
Exogenous insulin or sulfonylureas lower glucose.
## Who may have it
People with access to insulin or glucose-lowering medicines.
## How it is diagnosed
Critical sample, C-peptide, insulin antibodies and drug screening.
## Treatment
Safety, exposure removal and psychological support if appropriate.
## Food and eating
Regular plan with rapid glucose available.
## What to do during an episode
Measure glucose and take rapid glucose if low.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Which exposure was found?
- Did C-peptide or screening confirm origin?
- Which safety support is needed?
## More information
Endocrine and mental health teams.
