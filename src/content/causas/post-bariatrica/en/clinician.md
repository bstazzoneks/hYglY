<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: postprandial.</strong> It may occur one to four hours after eating, especially after gastric bypass.</aside>

## Clinical summary
It is postprandial hypoglycemia after bariatric surgery with altered hormone responses.

Confirm low glucose and avoid attributing all symptoms to dumping.

## Definition
True hypoglycemia requires compatible episodes and simultaneous low glucose.

Diagnosis should identify timing relative to surgery and exclude other causes.

## Epidemiology
Frequency varies by procedure and diagnostic criteria. Gastric bypass has the strongest described association.

## Pathophysiology
Accelerated gastric emptying and incretin responses may intensify insulin secretion.

An initial rapid fall may be followed by late hypoglycemia.

## Clinical presentation
| Pattern | Consideration |
|---|---|
| One to four hours | Postprandial hypoglycemia |
| Early after eating | Early dumping |
| Neuroglycopenic episodes | Requires urgent confirmation |
| Fasting reactions | Review other endogenous causes |

## Diagnostic criteria
Apply Whipple's triad. Measure low glucose with symptoms and confirm improvement after correction.

Interpret insulin and C-peptide only from a critical sample.

## Tests and cutoff values
No procedure-specific cutoff values are listed because no universal threshold is cited for all operations.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Serum glucose | Confirms episode |
| Insulin and C-peptide | Suggest endogenous source |
| Beta-hydroxybutyrate | Assesses ketogenesis |
| Supervised mixed-meal test | Studies postprandial response |
| Drug screening | Excludes exposure |

## Differential diagnosis
Early and late dumping, gastroparesia, insulinoma, sulfonylureas and factitious hypoglycemia.

## Management
Distribute carbohydrate and combine with protein and fiber. Individualize by operation.

Medicines require endocrine-bariatric follow-up. Surgery is rare and multidisciplinary.

## Complications and prognosis
Loss of consciousness, falls, food restriction and fear of eating. Symptoms may persist.

## Follow-up
Record glucose, symptoms, surgery and medicine changes. Refer for severe recurrence.

## Special populations
Adapt for pregnancy, pediatric patients and older adults. Review nutrition and fall risk.
