<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> It may follow eating when medicine does not match absorption.</aside>
## What it is
A medicine may lower glucose too much or act later than food.
## Quick summary
- Insulin and secretagogues may cause episodes.
- Gastroparesis may change absorption.
- Alcohol increases risk.
- Glucose should be measured during symptoms.
- Do not adjust doses alone.
- Keep a record.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, confusion or loss of consciousness.
## Why it happens
Medicine may remain active or food may be absorbed too quickly or slowly.
## Who may have it
People using insulin, sulfonylureas or meglitinides with schedule changes or gastroparesis.
## How it is diagnosed
Review dose, timing, food and glucose. Confirm Whipple's triad and exclude other mechanisms.
## Treatment
The team adjusts medicine and timing. Do not stop treatment without advice.
## Food and eating
Keep the advised schedule. Avoid alcohol without food and prolonged fasting.
## What to do during an episode
If conscious, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes. Repeat once if still low.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for unconsciousness, seizures or inability to eat.
## Daily life
Do not drive during symptoms. Carry prescribed glucagon if directed.
## Questions for your doctor
- Does dose match my meal?
- Could gastroparesis affect absorption?
- Do I need medication adjustment?
## More information
Diabetes team and therapeutic education.
