## Clinical summary
Episodes should be attributed only after excluding medicines and organ dysfunction.

## Definition
No single post-transplant phenotype exists. Hypoglycemia and, when suspected, endogenous hyperinsulinism must be demonstrated.

## Epidemiology
Poorly defined. Data vary by organ and immunosuppressive treatment.

## Pathophysiology
Liver, kidney, endocrine or pancreatic dysfunction may exist. Medicines may alter insulin secretion or clearance.

## Clinical presentation
It may be postprandial, intercurrent or fasting. Presentation depends on transplant type.

## Diagnostic criteria
Apply Whipple's triad. Confirm low glucose and assess insulin, C-peptide and ketones.

## Tests and cutoff values
No specific cutoff values are listed. Organ-based studies are required.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Critical sample | Documents endogenous hyperinsulinism |
| Liver or kidney function | Identifies associated conditions |
| Drug screening | Excludes exposure |
| Specialized imaging | Only with clinical suspicion |

## Differential diagnosis
Insulinoma, medicines, adrenal failure, liver disease and factitious hypoglycemia.

## Management
Correct organ or medication cause. Do not change immunosuppressants without transplant team input.

## Complications and prognosis
Neuroglycopenic injury, rejection or harm from inappropriate medication changes.

## Follow-up
Coordinate transplant and endocrine follow-up. Document every episode.

## Special populations
Adapt for pregnancy, pediatric patients and older adults.
