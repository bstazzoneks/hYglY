<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: both.</strong> It may follow meals or fasting. Hyperinsulinism is not established for every transplant.</aside>

## What it is
After transplantation, episodes may have many mechanisms. These include insulin, adrenal hormones or liver function.

This record does not confirm a specific cause. Assessment depends on organ and medicines.

## Quick summary
- Specific evidence is limited.
- Medicines may change glucose.
- Food may interact with liver or kidney function.
- Glucose should be measured during symptoms.
- Common causes must be excluded.
- The transplant team should participate.

## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger or confusion. Weakness or loss of consciousness may occur.

## Why it happens
Mechanisms may include endogenous insulin, liver changes, adrenal failure or medicines.

Do not assume hyperinsulinism without testing.

## Who may have it
Transplant recipients may have multiple risk factors. These differ across kidney, liver and pancreas transplantation.

## How it is diagnosed
Apply Whipple's triad. Review insulin, C-peptide, ketones, medicines and kidney or liver function.

The transplant team should guide etiology.

## Treatment
Treat the cause and adjust food plans. Do not change immunosuppressants alone.

## Food and eating
Keep regular meal timing if advised. Avoid alcohol and fasting without direction.

## What to do during an episode
If conscious, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes. Recheck and repeat once according to the plan.

Call emergency services for loss of consciousness.

## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for seizures, persistent confusion or inability to eat.

## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms. Discuss exercise, alcohol and pregnancy with the team.

## Questions for your doctor
- Which transplanted organ may be involved?
- Which medicine could change glucose?
- Do studies confirm hypoglycemia?
- Is there hormone or liver failure?
- Which specialist should coordinate treatment?

## More information
- Transplant team.
- Endocrine specialist.
- Local emergency services.
