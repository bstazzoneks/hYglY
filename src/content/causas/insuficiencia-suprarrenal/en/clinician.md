## Clinical summary
Glucocorticoid deficit may present with hypoglycemia, hypotension and electrolyte disturbance.
## Definition
Primary or secondary adrenal insufficiency.
## Epidemiology
Hypoglycemia is more frequent in children and with concomitant deficits.
## Pathophysiology
Low cortisol reduces gluconeogenesis and lipolysis.
## Clinical presentation
Weakness, nausea, hypotension and hypoglycemia.
## Diagnostic criteria
Simultaneous low glucose and hormone confirmation.
## Tests and cutoff values
No proprietary values are listed.

| Test | Use |
|---|---|
| Cortisol and ACTH | Distinguish origin |
| ACTH stimulation | Confirms primary reserve |
| Electrolytes | Assess dehydration |

## Differential diagnosis
Hypopituitarism, sepsis, dehydration and fasting.
## Management
Glucocorticoid replacement and sick-day plan.
## Complications and prognosis
Adrenal crisis and death if untreated.
## Follow-up
Adjust dose and review associated axis.
## Special populations
Pregnancy and pediatric care.
