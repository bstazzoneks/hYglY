<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type of hypoglycemia produced most often: fasting.</strong></aside>
## What it is
The adrenal glands produce too little cortisol. Low cortisol can prevent appropriate glucose release.
## Quick summary
- It may occur with fasting or illness.
- It may cause low blood pressure and dehydration.
- Cortisol replacement may need sick-day adjustment.
- Do not stop treatment alone.
- Hormone studies confirm it.
- Crisis requires emergency care.
## Symptoms
Weakness, nausea, dizziness, confusion and sweating.
## Why it happens
Cortisol helps release liver glucose. Deficit reduces this response.
## Who may have it
People with autoimmune disease, bleeding, infection, surgery or hypopituitarism.
## How it is diagnosed
Measure glucose and review cortisol, ACTH and electrolytes.
## Treatment
Glucocorticoid replacement and illness plan. Changes require medical direction.
## Food and eating
Regular meals and hydration. Avoid prolonged fasting.
## What to do during an episode
If able to swallow, take rapid glucose and wait 15 minutes. Follow the cortisol plan.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for persistent vomiting, dehydration, low pressure or loss of consciousness.
## Daily life
Carry a medical card and prescribed glucagon if directed.
## Questions for your doctor
- What caused my adrenal insufficiency?
- Do I need sick-day adjustment?
- Is hypoglycemia confirmed?
## More information
Specialized endocrine care.
