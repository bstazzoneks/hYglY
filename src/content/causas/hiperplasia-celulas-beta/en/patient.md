<aside class="medical-type"><strong>Type: both.</strong> Postprandial episodes may occur but are not exclusive.</aside>
## What it is
A diffuse or focal increase in insulin-producing cells.
## Quick summary
- It may resemble NIPHS.
- Insulinoma must be excluded.
- Critical testing confirms it.
- Imaging may be negative.
- Treatment depends on specialist findings.
## Symptoms
Trembling, sweating, hunger, confusion and seizures.
## Why it happens
Beta cells secrete insulin without adequate control.
## Who may have it
People with recurrent hypoglycemia and negative tumor testing.
## How it is diagnosed
Supervised fasting, insulin, C-peptide and localization studies.
## Treatment
Diet, medicines or surgery may be considered by pattern.
## Food and eating
Frequent meals while under evaluation.
## What to do during an episode
Take rapid glucose if conscious and recheck.
## When to seek emergency care
Seek help for loss of consciousness or seizures.
## Daily life
Avoid driving with symptoms.
## Questions for your doctor
- Is there focal or diffuse hyperplasia?
- Which sample confirms endogenous origin?
- Could surgery help?
## More information
Specialized endocrine and pancreatic teams.
